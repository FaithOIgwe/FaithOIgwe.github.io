An app-to-loan funnel that counts each person once

The problem. The monthly funnel was built by adding up daily distinct users. Anyone who came back on a second day was counted twice, so the monthly numbers ran high and couldn't be compared.

05
Migo
Lending · Metric design
Shipped

One definition of "Direct Debit adoption"

The problem. The same phrase covered two different things: loans where Direct Debit was required at application, and loans where it was actually set up. Nobody could say cleanly whether it was working.

What I did. Wrote each measure down explicitly, separated first-time from repeat borrowers, and segmented first-timers by eligibility and whether a credit score existed, so each kind of offer is compared like for like.

The point. One agreed number to judge Direct Debit by, instead of a debate about whose figure is right.

07
Migo · Credit risk · Investigation

Sizing the damage from a credit-model bug

A scoring bug ran for months before it was fixed. The business needed to know what it had changed and who it had touched.

I built before-and-after monitoring of average offer size and offer-to-loan conversion, then traced the model's inference records to find the borrowers affected. "The model was off" became a named list Growth could go back to.

The 5-Minute Rule. My entry for FP20 Analytics Challenge 42, on an AI-agent customer-support dataset, built twice: once in Power BI and once in Tableau.

Same six steps, whether it's a quick question or a migration.

  1. 1 Question

    What decision is this answer for?

  2. 2 Metric

    Write the definition down before any SQL.

  3. 3 Grain

    One row per what? Get it wrong and nothing adds up.

  4. 4 Validate

    Dedupe, tie to a known total, test the edge cases.

  5. 5 Drivers

    Segment until the change has a reason.

  6. 6 Communicate

    Lead with the answer and what to do about it.
